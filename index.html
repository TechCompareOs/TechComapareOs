About TechCompare OS

TechCompare OS is a verified campus marketplace for Tshwane University of Technology (TUT) students. Many first-year students need a laptop or phone for their studies but cannot afford a new one, and buying second-hand on open social media groups exposes them to scams, fake listings and strangers who learn too much about them. TechCompare OS solves this by letting only students with a verified TUT email buy and sell devices, by showing a fair trade-in value for an old device, and by recommending specifications that match a student's course and budget.

Research: Evidence of the Problem

Five published cases show the fraud patterns that students face when buying and selling devices online. Each summary is in the group's own words, with the source named under it.

1

Fraud wave in eMalahleni

Mudzanani (2026), The Witness, 5 June 2026

What happened: Five separate fraud cases over four days in May 2026 cost victims about R700,000 in total. They included a buyer who paid for machinery advertised online while the seller disappeared, a fraudster posing as a bank official, and a supplier whose company had already been deregistered.

What it means for TechCompare OS: The online purchase failed because nobody could check the seller. Only students with a verified TUT email can list on our marketplace.

2

BogusBazaar fake webshops

Marx (2024), SRLabs Research, 5 August 2024

What happened: Security researchers found a network of more than 75,000 fake online shops, with about 22,500 active in April 2024. Since 2021 it has handled over one million orders worth an estimated USD 50 million, affecting more than 850,000 victims. Fake payment pages collected card details, and the goods usually never arrived.

What it means for TechCompare OS: A shop can look professional and still be fake. Our seller profiles show ratings and comments built up over real sales, which is harder to fake than a single review.

3

Macro Venture broker complaints

Egorov (2026), DNB Forex Review, 23 April 2026

What happened: The article describes a pattern seen with scam brokers: pressure to deposit more money, delays and extra fees when withdrawing, and support that goes silent after payment. It describes general patterns rather than confirmed cases, and the site also sells refund-help services.

What it means for TechCompare OS: Scammers use pressure and then disappear. Conversations on our platform stay in the in-app chat history, and there is no "limited-time deal" pressure built into the site.

4

Fake "Students Laptop Scheme 2026"

Khourie (2026), Africa Check, 17 April 2026

What happened: WhatsApp messages promised free laptops to more than 960,000 students and sent people to a registration link. Africa Check's antivirus flagged the link as phishing, and the article rated the message a scam. It is a recurring hoax that keeps coming back in new forms.

What it means for TechCompare OS: Students who cannot afford a laptop are an easy target. TechCompare OS only asks for your TUT email, never your ID number or bank details.

5

Student-targeted scams

Scheepers (2024), Parys Gazette, 26 November 2024

What happened: Students looking for cheap tech, and students selling laptops or consoles, are both targeted. Warning signs include very low prices, buyers who offer more than the asking price, requests for extra courier fees, fake payment messages, and pushing the chat onto private messaging apps. The advice includes meeting in safe public places and not letting anyone rush you.

What it means for TechCompare OS: Buyers and sellers chat inside the platform, and the Trade-In Estimator gives a fair price to compare against.

What we learned

Every case involves a seller or shop that cannot be checked, urgency, and a payment that cannot be recovered. TechCompare OS responds with three things: verified student accounts, seller profiles with ratings and comments, and in-platform chat. Full references are on the Sources page.
